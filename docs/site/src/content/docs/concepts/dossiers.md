---
title: Stations and dossiers
description: Labels and scenes as places on the map — who is there, when, and where on the sky.
---

Some stars belong together for a reason the similarity graph does not know: they
released on the same **label**, or they come from the same **place**. Those are
the sky's stations, and each opens into a **dossier** — a page over the sky that
answers the same three questions for any station, and then points onwards.

## What a dossier shows

- **Where on the sky.** A small map of the whole sky with every member of the
  station marked on it. This is the dossier's argument rather than an
  illustration: a label's roster or a city's people either gather in one part of
  the sky or scatter across it, and which one it is says whether the station is
  a sound. **Mark them on the sky** closes the dossier, rings every member in
  gold on the big map, and frames them — leaving out the few far from the rest,
  so the cluster fills the screen. A small panel names the station while the
  rings are up, leads back to its dossier, and takes the rings away.
- **When.** Year by year: for a label, its output — every official release, not
  only those by artists the canon holds, because this is the label's history;
  for a place, the year its acts began.
- **Who.** The roster, each member with the years they span drawn on the same
  axis as the chart above it, so a bar lies under the history it is part of. A
  label's roster is ranked by releases on the label; a place has no weight of its
  own, so its people are ranked by how bright they are on the sky.
- **Its sound**, as the roster's main styles — the same "main" the names on the
  sky and the radio use.
- **Where next.** A label points to its owner, its imprints and the places its
  roster comes from; a place points to the labels its people released on.

A name in a dossier opens its star: the dossier closes and the sky flies there.

## Labels

A label is a **Discogs** label. Its roster is every artist on the sky with an
official release on it, from the Discogs releases file: bootlegs name labels
that never agreed to them and are not counted, nor is "Not On Label", which is
Discogs saying there was none. Pressings count — a label that issued an album in
twelve countries did issue it twelve times.

The description is Discogs's own. Where it names an artist the canon links to
that Discogs entry, the name opens the star; where it names another label that
has a dossier here, the name opens that.

A card lists the labels its artist released on, most releases first, with the
years of the first and last release on each. Each opens the label's dossier.

## Scenes

A scene is a **place of origin**, as Wikidata records it: where a group formed,
where a person was born. These are different claims — the band that formed in
Manchester made its music there; a singer born there may have made theirs
elsewhere — so a dossier counts them apart, and each member says which.

On a card, "formed in Manchester" opens Manchester.

Places are as fine as Wikidata's: Brooklyn is its own scene beside New York
City, and Madchester is Manchester.

## Finding a station

The search box finds labels and places as well as stars, each under its own
heading — "Motown" the label and "Motown Sound" the act are different answers.
The kind holding the exact name typed comes first: "Detroit" leads with the
place, "Motown" with the label.

## Addresses

A dossier has an address of its own, and it opens over the sky:

| Address | Opens |
| --- | --- |
| `/label/1` | The label with Discogs id 1 |
| `/scene/Q18125` | The place that is Wikidata item Q18125 |

The camera's fragment still works after it — `/label/1#-12.4,88.1,6` — because
the sky is behind the page.
