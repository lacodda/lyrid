In the [exploration mode](/lyrid/concepts/two-modes/) the sky starts dark and you light it yourself, by listening.

## Four states of a star

| State | What you see | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Open** | A star at full strength with its name beside it, drawn at every zoom | Everything: it is listed in view, and its card says when you lit it |
| **Lit** | A star in colour, because an open star is near enough to light it | Click it, read it, listen to it |
| **Echo** | A dashed ring: one of the three closest unheard stars of an open one | Click it or follow it from the card of the star that reflects it |
| **Dark** | A faint grey silhouette | Nothing yet. A click picks nothing, and the list of the view does not name it |

The silhouettes stay visible so the dark keeps its size and its shape. Regions you have not been near read **uncharted** where a genre would be lettered, and the compass says so as well.

## What opens a star

- **Thirty seconds of sound** from the player on the sky, whether you pressed play on the star's channel or a radio or the signal reached it. Only time the player reports as playing counts: a pause, a stall or a channel that will not play adds nothing.
- **Following a link out** to hear them, from the star's card. It is what there is for the half of the sky with no channel that embeds, and lyrid remembers that this is how the star was opened.
- **Your listening elsewhere**, read from a [linked ListenBrainz account or a Last.fm file](/lyrid/concepts/scrobbling/). Linking lights everyone you have ever heard.
- **Naming an artist you love**, once, when the [expedition](/lyrid/concepts/the-expedition/) begins. That star is your home port, and lyrid remembers it was named.

Search and the card are not hidden. You can look up any artist, fly there and read about them, and the star stays dark until you listen.

## Echoes

An open star reflects its three closest unheard stars, by the same similarity the sky was laid out from. Open one of those and the next-closest takes its place, so there is always a next step in sight. The list of the view gives the keyboard the same way forward: open stars by name, then echoes without one, until they are reached.

## Before you have an account

Without an account you see the whole sky, and what you listen to is remembered in your own browser, by MusicBrainz id, on nothing but this machine. When you sign in, at registration or to an account you already have, those stars move into it and the browser forgets them. If you then choose the creative mode, which keeps no progress, they are dropped.

## When the canon changes

Your progress is kept by MusicBrainz id and not by anything the canon numbers itself, and none of the light is stored, so a sky that is built again and laid out differently lights the same stars in their new places. Two kinds of change need a closer look:

- **Merged.** When MusicBrainz merges an artist into another, your opening and your listens move to the star it became, keeping the earliest date you opened either of them.
- **Gone.** When an artist is removed outright, your opening stays where it is. It lights nothing today, and it will again if the canon ever holds that artist again.

Your profile records [which edition](/lyrid/concepts/what-changed-in-the-sky/) of the canon your progress has been carried into.
